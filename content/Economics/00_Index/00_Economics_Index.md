## Definition

There is only finite resource on earth for all the infinite needs. These resource includes all the tangibles (machinery, land, inventory,  precious metals) and intangibles (IP, labor, brand reputation).

Economics is the study of how societies allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants. It explains how individuals, firms, and governments make decisions under conditions of scarcity, and how these decisions interact to determine prices, output, income, and welfare.

The discipline combines theoretical reasoning, mathematical modeling, and empirical analysis to understand both individual behavior and aggregate economic outcomes.

# 1. Core Structure of Economics

Economics is divided into three major interconnected branches:

## 1.1 Microeconomics (Individual Decision-Making)

Microeconomics studies how households and firms make decisions and how these decisions determine market outcomes.

### Core Areas:
- [[00_Microeconomics_Index]]
- Consumer behavior and utility maximization
- Firm production and cost structures
- Market structures and strategic interaction
- Welfare economics and efficiency analysis

### Focus:
- Prices and allocation of resources
- Individual optimization under constraints
- Market equilibrium at the micro level

---

## 1.2 Macroeconomics (Aggregate Economy)

Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole, focusing on aggregate indicators and policy responses.

### Core Areas:
- [[00_Macroeconomics_Index]]
- GDP and national income accounting
- Inflation and price level dynamics
- Unemployment and labor markets
- AD-AS and IS-LM macroeconomic models
- Fiscal and monetary policy analysis

### Focus:
- Economic growth and business cycles
- Stabilization policies
- Inflation and employment trade-offs

---

## 1.3 Econometrics (Empirical Validation)

Econometrics provides the statistical tools to test economic theories using real-world data.

### Core Areas:
- [[Regression Analysis]]
- Causal inference and hypothesis testing
- Time series and forecasting (future expansion)
- Demand and macroeconomic estimation models

### Focus:
- Converting theory into measurable relationships
- Testing validity of economic models
- Data-driven policy evaluation

# 2. Core Foundational Concepts

These concepts form the philosophical and analytical foundation of all economic reasoning:

- [[Scarcity]] — limited resources versus unlimited wants  
- [[Opportunity Cost]] — value of the next best alternative foregone  
- [[Supply and Demand]] — fundamental market interaction mechanism  
- [[Equilibrium]] — point where opposing forces balance in markets  

These ideas appear repeatedly across microeconomics, macroeconomics, and econometrics.

---

# 3. Mathematical and Analytical Tools

Economics relies heavily on formal modeling and quantitative reasoning:

- [[Functions]] — representing relationships between economic variables  
- [[Optimization]] — modeling decision-making under constraints  
- [[Regression Analysis]] — estimating relationships using data  

These tools allow economists to:
- Formalize consumer and firm behavior
- Analyze equilibrium conditions
- Test theoretical predictions empirically
